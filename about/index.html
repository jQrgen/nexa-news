About Nexa News

Nexa News collects public news, posts, releases and discussion about the Nexa blockchain on one page, with a short summary in our own words and a link to the original.

Disclosure. Nexa News is run by Jørgen S. Notland (jQrgen) in Oslo. jQrgen works on Nexa with Bitcoin Unlimited, the organisation that develops the Nexa full node. That means this site is not neutral. Items are labelled Official when they come from Nexa or Bitcoin Unlimited channels (including jQrgen's own articles) and Independent when they come from anyone else. Criticism with a source is included.

How it works

  • Collected by software, checked by people. Once a day a script reads public feeds (RSS and Atom), a public news search and the nexa.org sitemap, listed on the Sources page. It follows robots.txt and each platform's terms, waits between requests and never reads text behind a paywall or login.
  • AI-assisted. AI assistants help find items and draft summaries. An editor reviews every item before it appears, and jQrgen is responsible for the site.
  • Our own words. Each item shows the title, the author or account, the platform, the date, a link and a one or two sentence summary we wrote. We never copy whole posts. Read the full text at the source.
  • What we leave out. Price talk, price predictions, paid promotion, shilling and spam are flagged by the software and left out unless the editor finds real news in them. Nothing here is investment advice.
  • X (Twitter). X has no free API and scraping breaks its terms, so we don't read X automatically. Notable posts are added by hand and shown as a plain link with our own summary. We load no X widgets or scripts.
  • No tracking. No cookies, analytics, ads, web fonts or third-party scripts. Links go straight to the source.

Nexa News editorial policy (approved by jQrgen, 2026-10-03)

Status: approved by jQrgen on 2026-10-03.

Disclosure (shown at the top of every page and on the About page)

Nexa News is run by Jørgen S. Notland (jQrgen), who works on Nexa with Bitcoin Unlimited. The site collects public material about Nexa from across the internet, including criticism. It isn't an official Nexa channel and nothing on it is investment advice.

What we publish

  • Public material about the Nexa blockchain, such as news articles, blog posts, GitLab releases, forum threads, Reddit, Medium, YouTube, podcasts, X posts (added by hand as links) and exchange or listing announcements.
  • Every item has a working link, the source and author, the date, and a short summary in our own words. We never copy whole posts or articles.
  • Criticism is included when it has a source we can link to. We describe it fairly and don't answer it on the item itself.

What we don't publish

  • Price talk, price predictions, trading tips or shilling.
  • Paid promotion, sponsored posts or press releases presented as news. If we can't tell whether a piece was paid for, it stays out until we've checked. If an item is newsworthy and clearly marked by its publisher as sponsored, it can run only with a visible "Sponsored" label.
  • Anything we can't link to, unverified accounts, or rumours.
  • Material behind a paywall or a login, or anything collected in a way that breaks robots.txt or a platform's terms.

Sources

  • Official sources are the channels linked from nexa.org.
  • Independent coverage is listed in its own section, with a note on who runs each outlet.

Process

  1. The researcher finds items and flags price talk, promotion and unclear sponsorship.
  2. The editor checks every link and summary and approves or rejects each item.
  3. jQrgen gives final approval before anything is published.

Corrections

Errors are fixed openly. Each corrected item gets a dated "Corrected" note that says what changed.

Nexa News coverage plan

Status: draft by the editor, 3 October 2026. Not committed or published. Needs jQrgen's approval. Applies to: Q4 2026. Times are Oslo time (CEST until 25 October, CET after).

Standing rules (from editorial-policy.md, approved 2026-10-03)

  1. No price talk or shilling. No price predictions, trading tips or "next 100x" pieces. Items flagged price or shill are cut, or the price part is left out of our summary.
  2. Sponsored material is labelled or excluded. If we can't tell whether a piece was paid for, it stays out until we've checked. A newsworthy piece that its publisher clearly marks as sponsored can run, but only with a visible "Sponsored" label. Bitcoin.com News runs paid press releases, so check every item from it.
  3. Criticism runs when it has a source. If we can link to it, we include it and describe it fairly. We don't answer it on the item.
  4. Summaries are in our own words. Each item gets a short summary with a working link, the source and author, and the date. We never copy whole posts.
  5. Nothing without a link. No unverified accounts, no rumours, nothing behind a paywall or login, and nothing collected against robots.txt or a platform's terms. X, Reddit and the official YouTube channel are added by hand as links only.
  6. Disclosure. jQrgen works on Nexa with Bitcoin Unlimited. His own posts (Medium, X) are marked as written by the site's owner.
  7. Past events are archived, never deleted. Once an event has happened, it moves from the calendar or upcoming list into a "Past events" archive section, with its date and source link left as they were. If the outcome is public (vote results, a recording, a recap), add a link to it. This rule comes from the editor's brief and is not yet in editorial-policy.md, and the site doesn't have an events section yet.
  8. Process. The researcher flags items, the editor checks every link and summary, and jQrgen gives final approval before anything is published. Corrections get a dated "Corrected" note.

Beats

Cadence is based on what's in data/items.json and the forum.nexa.org topic list as of 3 October 2026.

1. Core protocol and the Tailstorm hard fork (Hard Fork 2)

  • Sources to watch: Nexa Forum (forum.nexa.org, nexa-forum), full node releases and tags on GitLab (gl-nexa-releases, gl-nexa-tags), spec.nexa.org (spec, which has a page for Hard Fork 2), the nexa.org blog (nexa-blog) and the nexa.org/roadmap page, plus the Bing News queries for Tailstorm (bing-news).
  • Cadence: full node releases are irregular, roughly every 6 to 12 months (2.0.0.0 in Feb 2025, 2.1.0.0 in Aug 2025, 2.2.0.0 on 1 Sep 2026). Tailstorm explainers and the monthly development updates fill the gaps.
  • Worth running: a new node release, an announced activation date or block height, an upgrade notice for node operators, exchanges or miners, changes to the spec, and bug reports with a public thread (for example "Full node 2.2.0.0 problem"). An activation date only counts as confirmed once the team announces it. A date that only shows up in a spec or code default goes to "to watch".

2. NexaPoW 2.0 and Blitz hardware

  • Sources to watch: the Nexa Forum "Hardware projects" category, the monthly highlights and newsletters, the BU forum (bu-forum, for example Peter Rizun's BUIP 225, which sets out his goals for Blitz and NexaPoW), spec.nexa.org (Mining, NexaPOW) and nexa.org/roadmap (the "Blitz Acceleration" items).
  • Cadence: the hardware article series has run every 2 to 4 weeks (27 Jul, 11 Aug, 9 Sep and 29 Sep 2026), and the fourth part says more is coming. NexaPoW 2.0 is part of the 2026 hard fork.
  • Worth running: new articles in the series, prototype or benchmark results with a source, availability or pre-order news for Blitz cards (check for sales language), and mining algorithm changes with a date. Leave out mining-profit and "how much will I earn" videos.

3. Wallets: Wally and Otoplo

  • Sources to watch: Wally Wallet releases on GitLab (gl-wally; the feed now redirects to gitlab.com/wallywallet/wallet), Otoplo releases (gl-otoplo), Wally posts on the Nexa Forum (the official "Wally" account), and the Bing queries for "Wally Wallet" and "Otoplo".
  • Cadence: Otoplo releases every 1 to 4 months (most recently v5.3.0 on 22 Sep 2026). Wally has a release or beta roughly every 1 to 3 months (3.30 beta in Jun, 3.30.02 in Jul, 3.31.00 on 9 Sep 2026).
  • Worth running: releases with user-facing changes, security fixes, feature explainers (P2P offers, Web3 login), and support for Tailstorm or Hard Fork 2. Most YouTube wallet how-tos are in Hindi or Urdu. They stay pending unless they add something and we can summarise them accurately.

4. Developer tools: libnexa-js, libnexakotlin, Rostrum, NexScript, Build On Nexa

  • Sources to watch: libnexa-js tags (gl-libnexa-js), libnexakotlin releases (gl-libnexakotlin), Rostrum releases (gl-rostrum), build.nexa.org (build-on-nexa, which has no feed, so watch for BON news on the forum), the Nexa Forum "Developers" category, the dev.to tag (devto) and GitHub search (github-search).
  • Cadence: libnexakotlin releases every few weeks to months (0.5.66 on 11 Jun, 0.5.68 on 25 Jun, 0.7.0 on 4 Sep 2026). Rostrum releases once or twice a year (latest 14.0.1 on 3 Apr 2026, though the 2.2.0.0 post says Rostrum was "updated alongside" it, so look for a new tag). libnexa-js has had no new tag since Sep 2024. NexScript and BON news arrives about monthly on the forum.
  • Worth running: releases, breaking changes, new SDKs (the Python library is "coming soon" on the roadmap), the AI skills or Starter App, playground upgrades, and serious third-party repos. Leave out repos that call themselves "official" without being linked from nexa.org.

5. Bitcoin Unlimited governance and votes (VotePeer)

  • Sources to watch: the BU forum (bu-forum), bitcoinunlimited.info (bu-site), the VotePeer group on GitLab (gl-votepeer, no feed, add by hand) and @BitcoinUnlimit on X (x-bu, by hand).
  • Cadence: BU votes come a few times a year (Apr 2025, Nov 2025, Feb 2026 and 9 to 14 Oct 2026), each announced about five weeks ahead. Officer elections follow two-year terms.
  • Worth running: vote announcements, each BUIP put to a vote, the vote thread, and the results. Report results as the forum states them. Summaries stay neutral: the BU people involved (Peter Rizun, Andrew Clifford, Andrew Stone) are jQrgen's colleagues, so the disclosure matters most on this beat.

6. Ecosystem apps and games

  • Sources to watch: the Nexa Forum ("Ecosystem", "Community events" and "ScriptQuest" categories), nexa.org/roadmap (TokaDex, Nexa Warriors trading card game, prediction market, wrapped BTC, cold storage wallet), the YouTube Data API search (youtube-api), GitHub search and Medium tag nexa (medium-tag-nexa).
  • Cadence: irregular. The roadmap expects TokaDex "in 2026" and the trading card game "early in the second half of the year", with no dates given.
  • Worth running: launches, testnet or beta openings, and demos with a public link. Partner trading competitions and promotions (for example "Nexa x Origami: Trading Competition") count as promotion, so cover them only as plain facts, or leave them out.

7. Events and conferences

  • Sources to watch: the Nexa Forum ("Announcements", "Spaces" and "Community events" categories), @NexaMoney on X (by hand), the BU forum, and event organisers' own pages once Nexa's participation is confirmed by an official Nexa or BU channel.
  • Cadence: Spaces on X around anniversaries and milestones (the halving, 21 June), roughly monthly webinars with EvolvH3r and All Things Blockchain, BONFire developer calls, and one or two conference appearances a year (Australian Crypto Convention 2024, Blockchain Futurist 2025, TOKEN2049 2025, Bitcoin MENA 2025).
  • Worth running: confirmed appearances (who is speaking, where and when, with an official link), recordings, and recaps. Sponsorships are disclosed as sponsorships. A third-party mention alone (for example a partner's LinkedIn post) isn't enough to list an event. When an event is over, move it to "Past events" (rule 7).

8. Independent coverage and sourced criticism

  • Sources to watch: Bing News (bing-news), CoinDesk, Cointelegraph, The Block (feed only, paywall), Decrypt, Bitcoin.com News (check for sponsorship), Hacker News (hn), Mastodon #nexa, Medium tag nexa, dev.to, YouTube Data API search, and r/Nexa by hand (reddit). The research list still needs a search of other crypto news sites, independent blogs, podcasts and critical threads (for example on Reddit and BitcoinTalk).
  • Cadence: low. Most weeks bring nothing usable. Expect noise from other brands called Nexa (Nexa AI, Nexa Cards, the NXA token and others).
  • Worth running: reporting or analysis by someone named, critical pieces with specifics (user problems, delays, security or centralisation concerns, exchange delistings or closures such as Exbitron), and non-English pieces that add something new (with a translated summary and a language flag). Each outlet gets a note on who runs it. Leave out price videos, "next Kaspa" hype, and anything we can't verify.
